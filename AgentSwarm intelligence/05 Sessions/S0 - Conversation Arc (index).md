## The shape of the whole thing

The arc is a single argument with itself.

**S1 asked: is this real?** The pitch — "an app that runs many terminals with 100% peak optimization" — was pulled apart. The lag turned out to be a documented vendor bug. The vendor already shipped the dashboard. What survived was one recalled moment: a session sitting on a permission prompt, unnoticed. The process built a rigorous plan around that moment.

**S2 said: that plan is not my idea.** The reset. What the reviews had produced was defensible and small; the original vision was a *terminal*, not a notifier. The vision was restored, with the review's evidence kept as engineering notes rather than as constraints. Then a second product — Recall — came out of an unrelated question and immediately proved sharper than the question that produced it.

**S3 refused to let either be assumed.** Three checks were run against reality: an audit of prior work, a measurement of whether the target knowledge exists in real transcripts, and a competitive recon. Two beliefs died. The plans got smaller and truer.

## What this record is for

The sessions keep the reasoning, including the parts that were wrong. Three specific reversals are preserved on purpose:

1. A demo built on knowledge that turned out not to exist in the data (S3)
2. A differentiation claim disproven twice by named competitors (S3)
3. A review process that produced a correct-but-wrong answer, and was overruled (S2)

Conclusions alone would hide all three.
